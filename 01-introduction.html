1  Introduction

In this unit

By the end you will be able to:

  • define an EBV and explain what it is for;
  • name the six EBV classes and recognise how they appear on the portal;
  • explain why the EBVCube stores data as netCDF, and what the format adds;
  • find and inspect a dataset on the EBV Data Portal.

This unit fills in the concepts you will lean on for the rest of the tutorial: what an EBV is, why it settled on netCDF, and how datasets are organised. You need no software yet, we install ebvcube in Unit 2. For now, everything happens in your browser, on the EBV Data Portal.

1.1 What is an Essential Biodiversity Variable?

An Essential Biodiversity Variable (EBV) is a standardised, derived measurement that captures a key dimension of biodiversity and lets us monitor its status and change over time. The concept was proposed by Pereira et al. (2013) and is maintained by the Group on Earth Observations Biodiversity Observation Network (GEO BON) (Group on Earth Observations Biodiversity Observation Network, n.d.). EBVs harmonise many primary sources, field observations, remote sensing, and model outputs, into a common, analysis-ready format. They sit as an intermediate layer between raw observations and the indicators used for assessment, reporting, and decision making.

Think of an EBV as the biodiversity counterpart to an Essential Climate Variable: a small, agreed-upon set of variables that different groups can produce, compare, and combine because they follow the same definitions. That shared standardisation is what makes data from different projects, taxa, and regions consistent enough to track over time.

1.2 The six EBV classes

EBVs are organised into six classes, each capturing a different dimension of biodiversity:

Genetic composition

Genetic diversity and differentiation within and among populations.

Species populations

Where species occur and how abundant they are.

Species traits

Physical, physiological and behavioural characteristics of organisms.

Community composition our example

The diversity and mix of species within a community.

Ecosystem structure

The physical build and extent of ecosystems.

Ecosystem function

The processes and productivity that keep ecosystems running.

The dataset we use throughout this tutorial, Local bird diversity (cSAR/BES-SIM), belongs to the Community composition class.

Canonical classes vs. what you see on the portal

The six classes above are the canonical classification defined by GEO BON. On the EBV Data Portal you may not see all six at once; the portal lists only the classes for which datasets are currently available. You may also meet finer labels such as Species distribution or Ecosystem disturbance, which are sub-categories within the canonical classes.

Ecosystem services is not one of the six EBV classes. It belongs to a separate, complementary GEO BON framework, the Essential Ecosystem Service Variables (EESVs), which covers the benefits people obtain from nature rather than biodiversity itself. For the official classification, see the GEO BON definitions page.

1.3 Why netCDF? The EBVCube format

A single EBV has a value at every location (longitude, latitude), at several points in time, and for each biological entity measured. Stored as separate map images, that becomes an unwieldy pile of files with no built-in context. The Network Common Data Form (netCDF), long used in the climate sciences, instead holds it all in one self-describing file: the data travels together with the information that explains it, units, dates, entities, and projection.

The EBVCube is the standardised, netCDF-based data format defined for EBVs. It follows two established conventions, CF v1.8 (cfconventions?) (how coordinates, units, and time are encoded) and ACDD v1.3 (acdd?) (the attributes portals use to index datasets), plus a few EBV-specific rules set out in the EBVCube format specification.

Every data cube inside an EBVCube shares the same four dimensions, so each pixel carries four pieces of information, where, when, and which entity:

lon

Longitude, or the projected x-coordinate.

lat

Latitude, or the projected y-coordinate.

time

One date or a whole series: annual, decadal, scenario years, irregular.

entity

The biological unit: a species, a group of species, or an ecosystem type.

The entity dimension is what lets a single EBVCube hold data for many species or other biological units at once. Above these four dimensions, metric and scenario groups organise different biodiversity measures or future projections within the same file. We return to that structure in Unit 4.

1.4 Touring the EBV Data Portal

All published EBV datasets live on the EBV Data Portal. Throughout this tutorial we use one of them as the running example:

Worked example: Local bird diversity (cSAR/BES-SIM)

id = 1 Community composition DOI 10.25829/8grx36

Martins, I., Pereira, H., Navarro, L. (2022). Local bird diversity (cSAR/BES-SIM) (Version 1) [Dataset]. German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv) Halle-Jena-Leipzig. DOI 10.25829/8grx36

Open the dataset page and you will find everything that makes an EBV dataset FAIR (findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable):

  • a stable dataset ID (1) and a persistent DOI you can cite;
  • a structured JSON metadata record (title, abstract, EBV class, creators, license, entities);
  • the netCDF file itself, available for direct download (≈ 4 MB);
  • an interactive EBV Visualizer for exploring maps and temporal trends without downloading anything.

The three views you will use most are the Discover page (Figure 1.2), an individual dataset page (Figure 1.3), and the live EBV Visualizer. Click either screenshot to enlarge it.

1.5 Where ebvcube fits

The portal is where EBV data is published and discovered; the ebvcube R package (ebvcube2025?; quoss2022?) is how you work with it. It sits on both sides of the portal:

READ
Reading published data

Give ebvcube a dataset’s numeric ID and it downloads the file, lists the cubes inside, and reads, subsets, or maps them, pulling id = 1 into R in a couple of lines instead of a manual download and netCDF wrangling.

CREATE
Producing new data

Starting from your own rasters or model output, ebvcube assembles them into a valid EBVCube, ready to upload and publish on the portal with its own DOI.

Read on one side, create on the other: that round trip is the workflow in the Overview diagram, and the path the rest of this tutorial follows, one step at a time.

Checkpoint. You just inspected dataset id = 1 on the portal, its EBV class (Community composition), its DOI, and its file format (netCDF). If those three make sense, you are ready to install the tools in Unit 2.
